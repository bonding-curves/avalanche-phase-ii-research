# Why Avalanche Validator Economics Need a Redesign

Matias Antonio, Avalanche Foundation Chief Investment Officer, published this argument on April 2, 2026. The [Team1 series index](https://www.team1.blog/p/growing-the-avalanche-economy-a-conversation) lists the article, but its historical X address does not reliably open. This page is an analytical guide, not a reproduction of the article. Its account rests on a dated research capture; the original Foundation blog edition has not been recovered for comparison.

## What the article argues

Matias identifies four concerns: validator security relies on finite token issuance, C-Chain activity does not directly pay validators, inflation dilutes holders when rewards are untargeted, and low differentiation can push delegation fees toward zero. He proposes studying activity-linked revenue and incentives that recognize validator performance or tenure. These are the author's diagnoses and design directions, not measured effects established by this page.

## Why it belongs in the reading sequence

The [Foundation's research agenda](/Phase-II/Essential-Readings/The-Avalanche-Foundation-Unveils-its-Economic-Research-Agenda/) names validator economics as a research stream. This article states the specific security-funding and market-design questions behind it. The [validator-rewards study](/Phase-II/Essential-Readings/How-Do-Avalanche-Validators-Actually-Think-About-Staking-Rewards/) supplies a separate empirical model; it cannot by itself establish the article's long-run security or revenue claims.

For Milestone 1, the research must distinguish issuance, fees, delegation income, operator costs, and validator participation in observed data. For Milestone 2, each proposed funding or differentiation rule needs its own explicit mechanism, recipient accounting, and comparison against security and concentration outcomes.
