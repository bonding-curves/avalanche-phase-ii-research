# Avalanche Economic Modeling

## Purpose

Build a source-backed understanding of Avalanche economics and turn it into reproducible empirical analysis, explicit behavioral hypotheses, and decision-relevant dynamic models. This book applies the BCRG Modeling Methodology; it does not redefine the general method.

## Reading order

1. **Research mandate** — contract scope, deliverable boundaries, stakeholders, decisions, and disputed evaluation requirements.
2. **Avalanche system** — network architecture, staking, validation, delegation, rewards, fees, supply, governance, and ACP lifecycle.
3. **Actors and perspectives** — validators, delegators, the Foundation, builders, service providers, and relevant cohorts.
4. **Economic questions and hypotheses** — participation, duration, capacity, fees, returns, preferences, constraints, and market feedback.
5. **Evidence base** — meetings, essential readings, ACPs, documentation, repositories, communications, datasets, interviews, and their quality.
6. **Data landscape and EDA** — available tables, event semantics, grain, time coverage, identity, missingness, distributions, cohorts, and limitations.
7. **Source reproductions** — reported quantities, formulas, estimation procedures, figures, tables, uncertainty, and discrepancies.
8. **Validator behavior** — choices, sensitivities, operating constraints, participation, duration, capacity, and heterogeneity.
9. **Delegator behavior** — allocation, fees, expected returns, duration, risk, capacity, and validator selection.
10. **Two-sided market** — matching, feedback, identification, propagated estimation error, and equilibrium assumptions.
11. **Protocol and ACP mechanisms** — controllable parameters, environmental conditions, constraints, transition rules, and intended effects.
12. **Dynamic model specification** — states, policies, mechanisms, flows, metrics, invariants, and evidence classification.
13. **Experiments and PSUU** — scenarios, counterfactuals, sensitivities, uncertainties, goals, KPIs, and interpretation.
14. **Findings and recommendations** — supported conclusions, rejected hypotheses, limitations, open questions, and client-ready communication.

## Three evidence tracks

For every primary source or approved research question:

- EDA establishes what the available observations can support.
- Reproduction determines whether reported evidence and methods can be independently reconstructed.
- Dynamic modeling explores mechanisms and counterfactuals using only accepted inputs or clearly labeled provisional assumptions.

## Scientific boundary

The textbook must distinguish protocol fact, reported result, independently reproduced result, team hypothesis, provisional modeling assumption, simulation result, and recommendation. Temporal or structural proximity never turns one category into another.
